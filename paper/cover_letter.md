# Cover letter — Nature Cities submission

Dear Editors,

Please consider the enclosed manuscript, "The energy productivity of neighbourhood form: everyday access per unit of energy across England", for publication in Nature Cities as an Article.

The paper measures two quantities that are currently assessed in isolation, household energy and everyday access, for 178,353 of England's 178,605 Census Output Areas, and reports the ratio between them, a measure of energy productivity for neighbourhoods. Energy is metered rather than modelled, car travel is anchored to measured National Travel Survey mileage, and access is computed over the national road network. On foot, a flat-type neighbourhood reaches about 27 times the everyday amenities of a detached-type one; at each area's own car catchment the counts nearly converge, but the detached area closes the gap only through about three times the car energy, so a compact neighbourhood obtains about 3.9 times the access per kilowatt-hour.

When the energy axis is recomputed under the technology measures of the national pathway (insulation, heat pumps and electric vehicles, including the Climate Change Committee's Balanced Pathway advice), full deployment reduces consumption everywhere yet closes less than a third of the energy gap between forms and none of the access gap. A strategy evaluated on consumption alone therefore records success while leaving the structural component of the problem unmeasured. To our knowledge, no prior work quantifies everyday access per unit of measured energy at national scale; the closest antecedent, the mobility energy productivity metric, is a prospective planning score built on modelled travel times; the manuscript cites and distinguishes it.

The estimand is place-level, and the paper bounds its exposure to residential self-selection directly, including a coefficient-stability analysis. Three extensions are deliberately out of scope and identified as such: a mover-panel test of household-level selection, re-zonation beyond the census hierarchy, and spatial error-model cross-checks on the clustered inference.

All data inputs are open, and the full pipeline and analysis code are public. The manuscript is not under consideration elsewhere, and the authors declare no competing interests.
