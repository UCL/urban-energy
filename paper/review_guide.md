# Review guide — for Gareth, Steve, Daniel and Matteo

How to read and check the manuscript before submission to Nature Cities. The documents to review are `latex/main.pdf` (about 3,400 words main text, 6 figures + 1 table) and `latex/extended_data.pdf` (4 figures + 4 tables), with `cover_letter.md`.

## What the paper claims

1. **Access axis.** On foot, a pure flat-type area reaches about 27× the amenities of a pure detached-type one (jobs 52×, people 12×); the gap narrows with distance but stays open at 25.6 km (11–14×).
2. **Energy axis.** Total household energy (metered gas + electricity plus NTS-anchored car travel) per dwelling is 2.12× higher in detached areas; travel (3.07×) dominates, home energy is 1.60× falling to 1.17× at equal household size and floor area.
3. **The rate.** At each area's own car catchment the reachable counts nearly converge, but only through about 3× the car energy, so a flat-type area obtains about 3.9× the access per kilowatt-hour.
4. **Scenarios.** Insulation, heat pumps and EVs (separately, combined, and at the CCC Balanced Pathway 2040 uptakes) cut consumption everywhere but close under a third of the energy gap and none of the access gap.

## What to scrutinise (the judgment calls)

- **Travel construction** (Methods, "The energy axis"). NTS class totals are exact anchors; the within-class allocator uses cars-per-person (elasticity 1) and commute distance (0.30). The ownership elasticity is swept to zero (floor 2.50×). Is the description fair, and is the floor prominent enough?
- **Estimand framing** (Results "Robustness", Methods "Robustness and the estimand"). The paper claims a place-level profile, not a household treatment effect, and rests the argument on access and the rate because the home-energy Oster bound is weak (δ*≈0.30). Are you comfortable with this division of the burden?
- **The rate's construction** (Methods, "The rate"). Catchment radius and travel energy are deliberately dependent (both functions of driving distance). The conditioning variants (5.3×, 2.3×, median 3.0×) bound it. Does the "by construction, not a separate model" framing read honestly?
- **Insulation scenario assumption.** Certificate potential-to-current ratio as achievable fabric improvement (both terms modelled, so the performance gap cancels). Stated as a limitation; check you accept it.
- **Tone of the policy paragraphs** (Introduction paragraph 4, Discussion "two asymmetries"). These carry the critique of asset-level instruments; flag anything that reads as overreach.

## Mechanical checks

- Every result number in both TeX documents is a `\nepi` macro generated by the analysis (`stats/ledger.py` → `latex/numbers.tex`); a hand-typed result number is a bug. To regenerate after any analysis change, run the six stats scripts then `latexmk` (recipe in `submission_checklist.md`).
- Figures are built by `stats/argument_figures.py` and `stats/map_figures.py`. The in-figure editorial titles can be suppressed for submission with `NEPI_PLAIN_FIGS=1`; the default set currently keeps them. **Open decision: submit with or without in-figure titles** (Nature convention is without).
