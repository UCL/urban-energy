  Effect of a p10 → p90 shift in jobs-housing balance, holding
  dwelling-type composition and the confounds:
    scale        units      p10      p90   amenities    energy      rate
    OA         178,322     0.16     0.87        1.15      1.07      1.07
    MSOA         6,855     0.50     0.95        1.00      1.02      0.97

  Finding: at MSOA scale the rate moves ×0.97, so once dwelling-type
  composition is held, jobs-housing balance carries no independent signal. These
  data support the compact-form recommendation and do not support the mixed-use
  half of it either way.

  Caveats that govern how far this can be read:
   * At Output Area scale the measure is dominated by area size: a small
     area needs few jobs to reach parity, which is why detached areas score
     higher on it than flats. MSOA is the interpretable scale.
   * Balance is one facet of mixed use. It says nothing about whether homes
     and workplaces share a building or a street, which is what the planning
     term usually means.
   * The q1 column is *balance*, the wrong construct for mixed use: it
     marks down a high street with many food outlets and one surgery.
     Mixed use is richness and proximity, which the per-service walkable
     counts in access_profile already carry, compact areas reaching more
     of every type within 1,600 m. Read q1 as a note on evenness, not as
     evidence about mix.
